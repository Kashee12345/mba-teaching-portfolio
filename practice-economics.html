Managerial Accounting and Decision Making

Practice Economics Lab

Run the numbers for Harborline Wellness Studio, a fictional neighborhood studio offering small-group classes and one-on-one sessions. Change any input and every result, chart and worked calculation updates instantly.

▸ Instructor notes

Learning objectives

  1. Compute contribution margin, CM ratio, break-even and target-profit volume, and explain each in plain language.
  2. Measure risk with margin of safety and degree of operating leverage, and explain why DOL depends on the volume you pick.
  3. Compare a per-visit price with a membership using contribution per client, simple customer lifetime value and CLV:CAC.
  4. Evaluate a capital investment with NPV, payback and IRR, and explain when the three can disagree.
  5. Turn quantitative results into a clear recommendation for a non-finance decision maker.

How to assign it

  • Pricing memo (main task): a one-page memo to the studio owner recommending whether to launch the membership, at what fee, and what churn rate would change the recommendation. Require one screenshot and one "show the math" calculation reproduced by hand.
  • Break-even warm-up: students answer the three "Try this" prompts on Tab 1 and post which change hurt break-even most and why.
  • Investment discussion: assign the back-loaded cash flow prompt on Tab 3 and ask students to defend or reject the purchase when payback and NPV point in different directions.
  • Grading focus: correct method, stated assumptions, sensitivity (what would flip the decision), and a recommendation that follows from the numbers.

Common student errors

  • Including fixed cost per unit in variable cost, which double counts rent and salaries.
  • Reporting 553.8 break-even visits instead of rounding up to 554; you cannot sell part of a visit.
  • Reading DOL as a constant. It falls as volume rises above break-even.
  • Mixing monthly churn with annual churn, or using revenue instead of contribution in the CLV numerator.
  • Treating a high CLV:CAC as always good. Above about 5:1 the studio may be underinvesting in growth.
  • Entering the discount rate as 8 instead of 0.08 when working by hand, or discounting the year 0 outlay.
  • Preferring the shorter payback when NPV says otherwise; payback ignores cash after the cutoff and the time value of money.

The lifetime value model used here

CLV = monthly contribution per client ÷ monthly churn rate
where monthly contribution = monthly revenue per client − (visits per month × variable cost per visit), and expected lifetime in months = 1 ÷ churn.

This simple model assumes constant churn and margin and ignores discounting, so treat CLV as an upper-bound planning figure rather than a valuation.
