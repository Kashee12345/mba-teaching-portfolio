Positive Leadership & Organizational Behavior

An evidence-based course for working professionals who lead people in clinics, studios, gyms, wellness programs, sport organizations and small businesses. We pair the core science of organizational behavior with positive psychology, so you can build teams where people do excellent work and want to stay.

  • 100% online
  • Asynchronous, optional live sessions
  • 10-week quarter
  • 5 quarter credit hours
  • Graduate level

Course description

Most leaders are promoted because they were good at the work, not because anyone taught them how people and groups actually behave. This course closes that gap. We study the established research of organizational behavior (motivation, personality, teams, culture, power and change) and read it alongside positive psychology and positive organizational scholarship, which ask a different question: not only what goes wrong at work, but what makes people and organizations flourish.

Across ten weeks you will work with Herzberg's two-factor theory, self-determination theory, expectancy theory, Seligman's PERMA model, character strengths, Cameron's positive leadership strategies, Edmondson's research on psychological safety, servant and transformational leadership, Tuckman's model of team development, Schein's levels of culture and Kotter's model of leading change. Every idea is tested against your own workplace. The course ends with a Leading Change Plan written for your own organization or one you know well, and a strengths-based reflection on who you are becoming as a leader.

Who this course is for

Working professionals in health, wellness, fitness, sport and small business: clinic directors, practice owners, head coaches, studio managers, program leads and anyone moving from expert contributor to leader of others.

How a week works

Each week opens Monday with a short video and lecture, readings, a self-check and a discussion. Initial discussion posts are due Thursday; replies and the applied memo are due Sunday at 11:59 pm in the course time zone.

Course learning outcomes

By the end of the course you will be able to:

  1. CLO 1Analyze individual and group behavior in a real workplace using evidence-based theories of motivation, personality, perception and engagement.
  2. CLO 2Apply positive psychology and positive leadership practices, including PERMA, character strengths and strengths-based feedback, to design interventions that support both wellbeing and performance.
  3. CLO 3Assess team development and psychological safety, and construct team agreements that improve collaboration and learning.
  4. CLO 4Evaluate servant, transformational, ethical and purpose-driven leadership approaches, and justify which fits a given situation, including decisions that involve data and limited resources.
  5. CLO 5Diagnose organizational culture using Schein's levels and design a staged change plan informed by Kotter's model and the course's positive leadership practices.
  6. CLO 6Formulate a personal leadership development plan grounded in assessed strengths, feedback and reflection.

Instructor presence

Online learning works best when you can feel the instructor in the room. Here is what you can count on.

Responses within 24 hours

Messages and Q&A forum posts get a reply within 24 hours on weekdays. Messages sent over the weekend are answered by Monday afternoon.

A weekly video

Every Monday, a short video opens the week: what matters, where students usually get stuck, and highlights from last week's discussion.

Feedback in 5 days

Memos, discussions and projects are returned with rubric scores and written comments within 5 days of the due date, so feedback arrives before the next similar task.

Optional live session

A 45-minute live session each week for questions, case discussion and team support. It is always recorded, and never required, so working schedules are respected.

Accessibility and Universal Design for Learning

The course is designed so that every student can reach the same outcomes by more than one path.

  • Multiple means of representation. Each lecture is available as text and as captioned video, with a transcript. Diagrams carry text descriptions.
  • Multiple means of action and expression. Applied memos may be submitted as a written memo or as a recorded five-minute briefing with an outline. Both use the same rubric.
  • Multiple means of engagement. Every assignment asks you to use your own workplace, and you choose the case for your final project.
  • Built to standards. Pages use real headings, keyboard-reachable controls, visible focus, sufficient color contrast and layouts that work on a phone.
  • Flexibility for working professionals. Each student may use one 48-hour extension on any weekly memo without explanation. Accommodations through the disability services office are honored in full.

Using AI tools responsibly

AI tools are part of the modern workplace, and learning to use them with judgment is a leadership skill. In this course:

Welcome uses

  • Brainstorming examples or counterarguments before you write
  • Explaining a theory in another way when a reading is unclear
  • Checking grammar and clarity of text you wrote yourself
  • Practicing a difficult conversation through role play

Ground rules

  • Your analysis and reflection must be your own work and your own voice
  • Add a one-line AI use note to any submission where you used a tool, saying what you used it for
  • Verify every factual claim and citation; AI tools can invent sources
  • Never enter patient, client, employee or confidential business information into an AI tool
Why this matters here. Many of you work under health privacy rules or hold client data. The habit of stripping identifying details before using any outside tool protects the people you serve and your organization.
