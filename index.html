How I teach

Plain language, then precision

  • Hard ideas told as a story first, then built up to the exact vocabulary and the numbers.
  • Examples drawn from the settings students actually work in: clinics, studios, teams and small firms.

Something to do every week

  • Cases, labs and games sit inside the reading so students test an idea the moment they meet it.
  • Applied memos ask students to make a real recommendation for their own organization.

Visible, positive presence

  • Replies within 24 hours on weekdays, weekly video, feedback within five days.
  • Feedback explains the reasoning behind a missed point, not just the right answer.

Built from experience

  • Six years leading nine faculty as a department chair, plus grant and program administration.
  • A clinician's view of wellness organizations and purpose-driven leadership.

Your instructor

Educator · academic leader · clinician

Dr. Akash Garg, D.C., MBA

I have taught since 2007, most of it to working adults and career changers, and online since 2019. For six years I chaired a college science department: nine full time faculty, more than forty courses, three campuses. That meant hiring, scheduling, developing faculty, rebuilding a catalog for online delivery and carrying a department through program review and accreditation. Before that I served as the college's Director of Grants.

My MBA and M.S. in Management and Leadership give that experience its framework; my M.Ed. in Learning Experience Design shapes how I build courses; and my Doctor of Chiropractic gives me an inside view of the health, wellness and sport organizations many MBA students lead or plan to start. Positive, purpose-driven leadership is not an abstraction for me. It is how I have tried to run teams and classrooms.

I build interactive tools like the ones on this site for my own courses, because adults learn what they can use the same week at work.
