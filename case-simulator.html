Instructor notes

Learning objectives

  1. Distinguish hygiene factors from motivators (Herzberg) and apply the difference to retention decisions.
  2. Explain how psychological safety, transparent communication, and servant leadership build trust during change.
  3. Apply Kotter's early change steps (urgency and a guiding coalition) to a real rollout that stalled.
  4. Use self-determination theory, strengths-based feedback, and PERMA to design sustainable engagement practices.
  5. Evaluate trade-offs among people, patient, and financial outcomes in a single leadership decision.

Module mapping

  • Best placed: the module on leading change and positive organizational behavior, after readings on motivation theory and before the change management unit closes.
  • Pre-work: short readings on Herzberg's two-factor theory, Edmondson on psychological safety, Kotter's 8 steps, Seligman's PERMA, and Deci and Ryan on self-determination theory.
  • Time: 15 to 20 minutes per play. Students should play at least twice and compare paths.
  • Decision 3 changes depending on the student's first move, so classmates will see different scenarios.

Using it as a graded discussion

  • Students post their leadership profile, final meter values, and answer the three reflection questions shown on the end screen (250 to 400 words).
  • Require citation of at least two named concepts from the case and one course reading.
  • Replies: respond to a classmate whose path differed from yours and argue which decision mattered most.
  • Suggested rubric (20 pts): concept application 8, evidence and citation 4, critical reflection on trade-offs 5, peer engagement 3.
  • Grade the reasoning, not the score. A student who reflects well on a poor path can earn full marks.
